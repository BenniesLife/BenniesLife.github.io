Hey folks! 👋

I've been digging into some pretty cool news from across the pond—Johannesburg is giving blockchain a try for water billing. Yep, you read that right. Instead of the usual spreadsheet nightmare, they’re using a blockchain platform to keep track of water usage, streamline billing, and (hopefully) make the whole process more transparent.

### What’s actually happening?

- **July 1, 2025** marked a big shift when Johannesburg Water took over the billing functions previously handled by the City of Johannesburg’s Revenue Shared Services Centre. This isn’t just a behind-the-scenes move; they’ve announced plans to integrate blockchain into the mix.
- The **Water Ledger** platform is one of the projects being piloted. It promises a “single source of truth” for water usage, ensuring all parties—from users to administrators—see the same data.
- Early anecdotes suggest the blockchain approach could cut down on disputes, reduce fraud, and make settlements faster, especially in high-density areas where manual billing often leads to errors.

### Why does this matter to us?

If you’ve been following Mistbits or Smarta, you’ll know how much emphasis we put on **IoT-driven water management**. Adding blockchain could be a game-changer for:

- **Real-time metering**: IoT sensors push data straight into the ledger, eliminating gaps.
- **Prepaid metering**: Residents can see exactly how much water they’ve used and pay accordingly, fostering conservation.
- **Cross‑network connectivity**: Whether it’s Sigfox, NB‑IoT, or LoRa, the blockchain layer can abstract the underlying connectivity, making the system more robust.

### A little side note

There was a brief mention of **prepaid meters** gaining traction in estates facing water scarcity. The city’s push for more accurate billing aligns with broader trends we’ve been watching across Gauteng—especially as climate pressures push municipalities to tighten water budgets.

### Bottom line

Johannesburg’s blockchain water billing experiment isn’t just tech for tech’s sake. It’s a pragmatic step toward **more reliable, transparent water services**—something that resonates with every smart‑water enthusiast out there.
