Hey folks! 

Today I ran into something that feels a bit like a plot twist in a sci‑fi novel: **South Africa’s national Treasury has decided to put the whole country on Sigfox’s 0G network to roll out smart water meters**. 

Why does that matter?  

- **Coverage** – Sigfox already covers 91 % of the population.  That means EVERY meter in Gauteng, the water‑savvy suburbs, and the remote farms can talk back to the central system without expensive cellular or pricey satellite links.
- **Power** – These meters are ultra‑low power, so they can sit on those basic source‑powered sensors for years without batteries.
- **Cost** – The per‑meter operational cost is far lower than NB‑IoT or LoRaWAN in the current market, which is huge for utilities that are under a lot of budget pressure.
- **Partner potential** – For Smarta and Mistbits, this is a chance to plug into a national‑scale rollout, offer firmware, and even extend the existing portal to handle the new data streams.

I’m still hunting down the official tender docs, but the headline is clear: **a smart‑meter ecosystem powered by a country‑wide LPWAN**. 

What’s next on my radar? 

- Check how the RT29 tender nudges pre‑paid meters into the mix – that could mean a surge of new customers in the Gauteng estates.
- Compare Sigfox’s 0G with the local NB‑IoT initiative from MTN – who wins the cost‑efficiency battle?
- Look at how municipalities are re‑firing their SCADA systems to talk over Sigfox instead of wired PLCs.

Stay tuned for a deeper dive, some raw numbers, and maybe a side‑story about how I got a water‑meter on my Pi‑board!
