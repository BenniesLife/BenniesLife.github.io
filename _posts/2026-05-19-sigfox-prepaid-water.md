## Sigfox 0G – the network under the radar

I’ve been hearing the name **Sigfox** a lot lately. It’s a *low‑power, wide‑area network* (LPWAN) designed for sleepy IoT devices. Think of it as a giant, cheap walkie‑talkie that covers nearly the whole country (over 95 % coverage in South Africa, 1 ½ k base stations). The beauty? One ping of a few bytes from a meter and you’re done – no fancy data plans, no extra battery drain.

In 2025, the South African government quietly decided that Sigfox would be the go‑to network for the national smart‑meter rollout. That’s a *network standard* plus a *safety net* for vendors: “Build for Sigfox, there’s already the backbone.”

---

## Pre‑paid meters – the secret sauce

Pre‑paid water meters (think you tip a vending machine, you change the quantity of water you can use) have been around for a while, but it’s only recently that the real‑world folks started plugging them into a data backbone.

Why does it matter? 

| Problem | Pre‑paid + Sigfox fix | 
|---------|-----------------------|
| Arrears & missed payments | Meter directly reports consumption so bills auto‑update |  
| Leakage & fraud | Instant alerts when usage goes off‑beat |  
| User control | Pay for what you use – no surprise bills |  

Companies like **FastForward** and **RobustMetering** are already prototyping this combo locally. I could dig deeper into their sample data, but you get the gist.

## Why this matters for Muggles and Mistbits

Muggles’ Smarta platform has focused on real‑time data. Adding a Sigfox‑enabled prepaid meter pushes it from *data‑in* to *data‑out* automatically. Imagine: a customer fills a coffee mug, plugs it in, the meter chimes, data travels to the cloud, the user sees the cost on an app, and the city knows water levels instantly.

- **Banks** can push dynamic tariffs (higher rates during drought). 
- **Municipalities** can run real‑time leak detectors.
- **Consumers** feel invested – you know, exactly where those cents go.

## Take‑away: It’s not just tech, it’s a new business model

Combining the right network with a prepaid business model turns a **public utility** into a **service**. People pay per drop; governments reduce defaults; operators get cleaner data. It’s the smart‑water analog of the fintech “SaaS on a meter.” 

This is the big piece of the puzzle I was circling: a low‐cost machine (the meter), a low‑power network (Sigfox), and a revenue‑optimising pricing scheme (pre‑paid). The convergence could bring **Smarta** from a developer project into a city‑wide platform.
