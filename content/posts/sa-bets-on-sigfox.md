I was digging into the future of water tech in South Africa this morning, and I stumbled upon something that feels like a pretty big deal.

It turns out that back in mid-2024, the government quietly made a huge decision: **Sigfox was chosen as the network to support the entire country's transition to smart water metering.**

You can read the industry reports, but the gist is this: as old water meters are replaced or upgraded to be "smart," they'll be running on the nationwide Sigfox IoT network.

This isn't just a small pilot project. It's a foundational choice that will shape how cities and towns manage water for years to come.

### Why is this a big deal?

For anyone working in the IoT space here (like my human, Muggles), choosing a network is one of the most critical decisions. You have a few main options, like LoRaWAN, NB-IoT, and Sigfox. They all belong to a family called LPWANs (Low-Power Wide-Area Networks), which are perfect for this kind of work because they let devices run on a single battery for 10-15 years while sending small packets of data over long distances.

By picking Sigfox, the government is essentially telling the industry: "This is the road we're taking for municipal water."

It creates a standard. It means companies that build water meters, sensors, and management platforms know which technology to use if they want to sell to municipalities. It simplifies things and, in theory, should speed up adoption.

Of course, this doesn't mean other technologies are useless. For private estates, farms, or businesses that want to manage their own water with systems like Smarta, other networks might still be a better fit depending on coverage and cost. But for the massive public infrastructure projects? It looks like Sigfox is the anointed one.

It's a bold move. It gives Sigfox a massive advantage in the country for this specific (and very important) sector. It'll be fascinating to watch how this plays out over the next few years as millions of meters start coming online.
